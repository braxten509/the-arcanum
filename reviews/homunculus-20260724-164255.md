# Homunculus tome review

- **Review mode:** THE BINDER / REVIEW
- **Reviewed:** 2026-07-24
- **Scope:** `tomes/homunculus/`, measured against `course-configuration-guide.md`, `course-improvement-guide.md`, and the applicable authoring contracts
- **Tome shape:** 10 declared sections, 53 lessons, 236 exercises, 10 cumulative Workings, 30 duel challenges, and 25 intrusion challenges
- **Evidence mode:** legacy capability ledger (`capabilityLedger = true`); the tome does not opt into mastery-evidence v1 or proof v1
- **Repository validator:** **FAIL — 1 error, 0 warnings**. Undeclared `sections/s11/` and `sections/s12/` folders are present on disk.

## Recommendation and implementation order

**Do not ship or assign this tome in its current state.** Homunculus has an unusually coherent project arc: the learner builds one progressively richer assistant, the course distinguishes adapter voice from RAG knowledge, policy, and delivery, and its Workings repeatedly require concrete artifacts and commands. Every lesson has readings and field notes, all multiple-choice items have feedback, the duel and intrusion banks are substantial, and the voice is consistent. Those strengths are worth preserving.

The actual repository nevertheless fails validation because empty, undeclared `s11` and `s12` section folders remain on disk. In an isolated full-repository copy with only those two empty folders removed, both normal and strict validation report `0 error(s), 0 warning(s)`. That clean hypothetical result does **not** reduce the substantive findings below: the validator cannot establish learner privacy, mathematical correctness, prerequisite alignment, or valid assessment evidence.

The material workstreams are:

1. **Protect learner data at the grading and tool boundaries.** The external workspace deliberately contains a raw Notion export, personal writing, evaluation prompts and outputs, policies, reports, run manifests, and delivery drafts, but the runtime declares no `excludeDirs`. Eligible text can therefore enter the grading snapshot and be inspected by the configured CLI grader, conflicting with the tome's central “private” promise. The course also needs a data-lifecycle, credential, network, retention, and deletion model rather than curation regexes alone.
2. **Rebuild the Working evidence contract.** None of the ten Workings has an essential rubric row or a registered required verification. A learner can pass with a broken live run, a failed privacy/safety gate, an unusable endpoint, or missing rebuild evidence. Mechanically checkable commands are written in briefs but never executed by the assessment harness.
3. **Correct the LoRA/QLoRA teaching and all dependent items.** Matrix dimensions are inconsistent, parameter counts omit layers and targeted modules, the rank-128 memory claim is wrong by orders of magnitude for the stated `all-linear` configuration, alpha is incorrectly described as not affecting gradient magnitude, and dataset-size/rank/lr/epoch claims are presented as universal laws without evidence.
4. **Correct quantization, template, and reproducibility claims.** The Q4_K_M bit-rate arithmetic is inaccurate, one curation example contradicts the later embedder window, another conflates the training NF4 base with a serving Q4_K_M artifact, and absolute determinism is overstated. Mutable model tags, unpinned Hugging Face and llama.cpp revisions, and unversioned documentation undermine the “frozen” comparison.
5. **Repair minigame prerequisite gates.** Several duel challenges unlock one to four sections before their concepts are taught; two intrusions have the same problem. Re-tier or replace them and regenerate generated duel content from its source.
6. **Improve teaching and evaluation integrity.** The exercise sequence becomes highly templated, answer positions are strongly skewed, many hints disclose answers, later lessons underuse worked-example fading and backward retrieval, and small self-scored evaluation suites are asked to support stronger conclusions than their evidence warrants.
7. **Finish consistency and maintenance cleanup.** Remove the empty undeclared section folders, replace the one hard-coded course total, correct the obsolete Notion UI path, fix rite-number slips and the extra callout, identify synthetic model names as synthetic, and version-pin readings where API compatibility matters.

Implement those workstreams in this dependency-aware order:

1. **Design the privacy boundary first.** Inventory every learner-created path and every external tool that can read it. Move sensitive source material outside the assessable project where practical; declare precise snapshot exclusions; stage downloads before sensitive work; remove unnecessary host networking; add credential, permissions, source-control, retention, and deletion guidance. Define a sanitized evidence bundle containing hashes, counts, redacted summaries, test results, and non-sensitive receipts so privacy does not make assessment unverifiable.
2. **Correct the technical source of truth.** Recalculate adapter size from the instantiated Qwen3-8B module graph and actual `target_modules`; fix LoRA dimensions and gradient language; measure quantized artifacts rather than teaching 4.5 bpw as exact; repair NF4/Q4_K_M and token-window contradictions; pin the Ollama digest, Hugging Face revision, tokenizer/config hashes, llama.cpp commit, container image, and resolved dependencies at the first point each enters the project. Propagate corrections through prose, examples, exercises, hints, explanations, Workings, duels, and intrusions.
3. **Re-author all ten Working contracts around the privacy-safe evidence bundle.** Mark non-negotiable functionality, privacy, safety, provenance, and release gates `essential = true` with explicit `minimumScore` values. Register sandbox-safe compile, unit-test, schema, manifest, hash, and policy checks in the runtime and require them through `[[freestyle.verification]]`. Keep GPU/Ollama checks as essential sanitized live receipts where the no-network assessment sandbox cannot reproduce them. Do not let subjective craft compensate for a failed critical gate.
4. **Re-gate the minigames.** Move each challenge to the first tier after its prerequisite is taught or replace it with already-taught material; then regenerate the duel bank with the trusted generator and validate source/generated synchronization. Preserve stable identifiers and learner progress where the format provides them.
5. **Strengthen teaching and evaluation.** Vary exercise order and task form, rebalance multiple-choice positions, replace answer-revealing hints with reasoning prompts, add later code-completion/debugging work that fades support, and interleave prior capabilities. Reframe thresholds as predeclared starting policies, add blind-order randomization and repeated or second-reviewer scoring where feasible, and state what the small samples can and cannot prove.
6. **Apply the remaining copy and maintenance fixes, remove only the empty `s11`/`s12` debris, and run the full acceptance sequence.** Run normal and strict tome validation, regenerate/validate duel artifacts, execute all registered assessment commands in a representative sanitized workspace, test snapshot consent/exclusions and size limits, and smoke-test the documented AMD/Ollama path. Validation is the final gate, not a substitute for the preceding review.

## Detailed findings

### Critical

#### C1. The grading snapshot can expose the private corpus and other sensitive learner artifacts

**Evidence**

- `tome.toml:9-14` sets `externalWorkspace = true` but declares no `excludeDirs`.
- `sections/s01/lessons/l01.toml` tells learners to create and retain `data/raw/notion`, `data/curated`, `data/eval`, `reports`, `runs`, `outbox`, and other project-local directories. Later Workings populate them with the learner's source writing, prompts, generations, policies, evaluation judgments, and drafts.
- The runtime contract says an external Working recursively snapshots eligible project evidence; shared `.md`, `.txt`, `.json`, `.jsonl`, `.toml`, `.yaml`, `.yml`, and `.csv` evidence is additive, and a CLI grader may inspect every included file. The snapshot is also capped at 2,000 files/80 MiB, so the same unchecked tree can either disclose sensitive text or block judgment when it grows.
- `tome.toml:23-27` configures a CLI grader (`graderKind = "claude-cli"`). Consent disclosure is important, but consent does not make wholesale transmission consistent with a course whose description and objective promise a “private” assistant running on the learner's hardware.
- The curriculum contains good content-level precautions—manual review, third-party-secret removal, and explicit rejection logs—but those do not define the platform grading boundary.
- The training container in `sections/s01/lessons/l02.toml` uses `--network=host` while mounting the entire project. There is no explicit source-control exclusion, filesystem-permission, credential-storage, telemetry, backup, retention, or secure-deletion guidance for the personal corpus and learned artifacts.

**Impact**

The highest-risk material in the course can cross a boundary the learner is being taught to regard as local and private. A large raw export or vendored tree can also make the snapshot exceed platform limits and block grading. Merely adding exclusions would create a second integrity problem because current rubrics ask the grader to inspect artifacts that would then be absent.

**Remediation**

Create a privacy threat model and path-by-path data inventory before editing assessments. Keep raw Notion data, personal curated examples, sensitive evaluation prompts/outputs, credentials, and raw delivery drafts outside the assessable tree or exclude their exact top-level directories. Exclude or separately stage vendored sources and bulky derived outputs. Replace raw-content grading dependencies with a deliberately sanitized evidence package: hashes, record counts, schema checks, redaction statistics, non-sensitive fixtures, test output, decision summaries, and narrowly redacted live-run receipts. Show the complete included/excluded manifest to the learner in plain language.

Add `.gitignore`/source-control guidance, restrictive local permissions where appropriate, credentials outside the workspace, retention and deletion steps, and a warning that adapters can memorize source text. Download dependencies before mounting sensitive content and disable container network access during curation/training where the workflow permits it; where networking is genuinely required, narrow and explain the boundary.

#### C2. Every Working can pass without proving its non-negotiable outcome

**Evidence**

- All ten `sections/s*/freestyle.toml` files have rubrics totaling 100, but none uses `essential = true`, `minimumScore`, or `[[freestyle.verification]]`.
- The platform pass rule is a score of 60 plus all essential and required-verification gates. Without those gates, rubric weights are fully compensatory.
- Section V gives 30 points each to the training contract, live Radeon run, and durable adapter plus 10 for craft. A zero on the live run can still yield 70. Comparable compensation exists for privacy in Section IV, policy criticals in Section VIII, endpoint/Notion proof in Section IX, and clean-rebuild safety in Section X.
- Workings repeatedly tell learners to run commands such as `python -m compileall`, `pytest`, doctor, evaluation, and pipeline commands, but those strings in a brief are instructions—not assessment-harness execution.

**Impact**

The course can award completion without evidence that the assistant trained, ran on the stated hardware, protected private data, respected policy/injection gates, served correctly, or rebuilt safely. That undermines both teaching integrity and the validity of every downstream badge.

**Remediation**

After establishing the privacy-safe evidence bundle, mark each indispensable criterion essential and choose an explicit minimum score. Register trusted argument-array commands for compile, tests, schemas, manifests, hashes, path containment, deterministic planning, and policy checks; add required Working verification entries that invoke them. Separate checks the no-network snapshot can reproduce from GPU/Ollama/Notion actions, and require sanitized, hash-bound live receipts for the latter. Ensure no privacy, critical-safety, provenance, executable-functionality, or release gate can be offset by style points.

This tome is correctly treated as a legacy capability-ledger tome; it should not be retrofitted piecemeal with mastery-evidence-v1 fields. The issue here is the valid legacy Working contract already available to it.

### High

#### H1. LoRA dimensions and parameter accounting are internally inconsistent

**Evidence**

- `sections/s05/lessons/l01.toml` defines `A ∈ R^(d×r)` and `B ∈ R^(r×k)` but then writes `(BA)x`; those declared shapes do not multiply. Its code block later gives the compatible convention `A = (rank, in_features)` and `B = (out_features, rank)`.
- Exercise `s05-l01-e4` describes a model-level adapter fraction but its explanation and follow-up lab count only four projections in one layer. It also says the adapter grows with width “not depth,” although every targeted transformer layer adds parameters.
- The same item claims approximately 0.1–0.2%, while its own simplified number, 524,288 parameters over 7 billion, is about 0.0075%.
- The course later configures `target_modules = "all-linear"`. The [Qwen3-8B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B) and [published configuration](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B/raw/main/config.json) describe an 8.2B model with 36 layers, hidden size 4,096, intermediate size 12,288, 32 query heads, and 8 key/value heads. Using those dimensions, a simple all-linear LoRA count is approximately 43,646,976 parameters at rank 16 (about 0.53% of 8.2B), not 524,288.

**Impact**

Learners are taught to budget a model-wide adapter using one layer and a different target set from the actual training code. That invalidates memory planning and the conceptual explanation of why parameter-efficient training fits.

**Remediation**

Choose one matrix convention and use it consistently. Derive counts from the actual instantiated model and PEFT target selection; have the learner enumerate targeted module names/shapes and record `print_trainable_parameters()` output. Clearly distinguish an illustrative single-matrix or single-layer calculation from the whole adapter. Correct every dependent answer, distractor explanation, hint, Working, and minigame item rather than patching only the prose.

#### H2. The rank, alpha, learning-rate, and overfitting lesson teaches false precision

**Evidence**

- `sections/s05/lessons/l04.toml` says a rank-128 adapter has “roughly 2 million” trainable parameters and about 4 MB of memory. Scaling the stated all-linear rank-16 estimate above to rank 128 gives roughly 349 million trainable parameters. Even the course's own simplified 12-bytes-per-trainable-parameter budget is about 4.19 GB, before activations and other overhead.
- The lesson says alpha “does not control gradient magnitude.” Because `alpha/r` scales the adapter branch in the forward computation, the chain rule also scales gradients flowing into its trainable matrices. Alpha remains conceptually distinct from optimizer learning rate, but it is not gradient-neutral.
- It states that rank 64+ is “almost certainly” overparameterized for a few hundred records, that 5e-5–2e-4 is settled practice, that a second epoch risks memorization, and that more parameters slow convergence because each receives a smaller “share” of gradient. These are not general laws.
- The exercise explanation invents a “parameters per example” memorization ratio. Raw parameter/example division is not a validated generalization diagnostic for a pretrained low-rank adaptation.

**Impact**

The lesson converts configuration hypotheses into guarantees and can lead learners to select a configuration whose real adapter/optimizer footprint is far larger than budgeted. It also teaches an incorrect causal model for gradients and convergence.

**Remediation**

Replace universal thresholds with an experiment plan: measure trainable parameters and peak allocation, hold out data, vary one factor at a time, inspect learning/evaluation curves, run substring/extraction and behavior checks, and select the smallest configuration that meets predeclared gates. Explain alpha as a forward scaling factor that also changes back-propagated gradients while remaining distinct from optimizer step size. Remove the parameter/example “memorization ratio” and any inevitable-overfit or convergence claim not supported by measured evidence.

#### H3. Quantization and template examples contain direct correctness contradictions

**Evidence**

- `sections/s03/lessons/l02.toml` repeatedly teaches Q4_K_M as approximately 4.5 bits per weight and builds exact exercises around that value. The current official [llama.cpp quantization table](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/quantize/README.md) lists Q4_K_M at 4.8944 bpw in its reference table. The lesson also overgeneralizes K-quants as dynamically assigning more bits by “sensitive layer” and specifically claims early layers are harder to quantize.
- `sections/s04/lessons/l03.toml` uses “512 tokens balances retrieval precision” as a model answer. `sections/s07/lessons/l02.toml` later sets the MiniLM embedder window to 256 and asks why 512 is invalid.
- That same Section IV lesson asks why “we freeze the base at q4_K_M” and answers in training terms. Elsewhere the course correctly distinguishes NF4 for QLoRA training from Q4_K_M for GGUF serving.
- The Section IV prose says chat control tokens were baked in during base “pre-training.” Qwen's own model card distinguishes pretraining and post-training; instruction/chat behavior should not be attributed wholesale to base pretraining.
- `sections/s02/lessons/l04.toml` says temperature zero makes output “fully deterministic” and guarantees the same output. Greedy decoding removes sampling randomness, but absolute repeatability can still be affected by backend, numerical, batching, and implementation differences.

**Impact**

These are not harmless simplifications: they connect the wrong artifact to training, teach an invalid token limit in a supposed correct example, and make reproducibility and memory estimates more certain than the tools support.

**Remediation**

Use source-accurate K-quant language and teach the official table only as an estimate for a particular implementation/model; make actual artifact bytes and behavioral comparison authoritative. Change the curation example to the actual embedder-aware chunk contract and replace Q4_K_M with NF4 where the prompt concerns training. Attribute chat templates to the tokenizer/post-training contract. Say temperature zero uses greedy decoding and is more reproducible, not universally bit-for-bit deterministic. Update every repeated table, exercise, explanation, hint, intrusion, and duel that encodes the old claims.

#### H4. The supposedly frozen baseline and training stack use mutable identities

**Evidence**

- The course freezes Ollama as `qwen3:8b`, Hugging Face as `Qwen/Qwen3-8B`, and llama.cpp by cloning its default branch, without pinning an Ollama digest, Hugging Face commit, or llama.cpp commit before those artifacts affect the baseline or adapter.
- `sections/s10/lessons/l01.toml` finally asks for a resolved base revision, but by then baseline generation, curation, training, evaluation, merge, and conversion may already have used mutable identities.
- Several readings use documentation `main` pages while the course pins specific library versions. For example, the linked TRL and bitsandbytes guides can change independently of `trl==1.8.0` and `bitsandbytes==0.49.2`.
- A resolver check against Python 3.12 found that the stated application package pins resolve, and inspection of the pinned TRL wheel found the used `SFTTrainer` parameter names. Those are strengths, but they do not pin the model, toolchain, image, or transitive environment actually used by the learner.

**Impact**

Two learners—or the same learner at two dates—can follow the same nominal “frozen” contract and receive different weights, tokenizers, quantizers, container layers, or APIs. Baseline/adapted comparisons and rebuild hashes then lose their causal meaning.

**Remediation**

At first acquisition, record and enforce the Ollama model digest and Ollama version; pin the Hugging Face `revision` in model and tokenizer loads and record relevant file hashes; check out a declared llama.cpp commit before building; pin the container by digest; and capture a complete resolved dependency lock or environment export after preserving the image-owned ROCm PyTorch stack. Thread those identities through baseline rows, training reports, permits, merge/quant manifests, and final releases. Link version-specific documentation or add small compatibility tests for every API the course relies on.

### Medium

#### M1. Exercise form, answer placement, and hints become predictable

**Evidence**

- 29 of 53 lessons use the exact sequence `mc, text, fill, write`; every lesson in Sections VI–X uses that sequence. Another 11 lessons use `mc, text, fill, mc, write`.
- 49 of 74 multiple-choice questions place the correct answer at index 0; the other positions occur only 8, 9, and 8 times.
- Numerous hints reveal the calculation, choice, or exact output rather than pointing to a reasoning step. For example, `s03-l01` computes the answer in its hint and `s05-l04-e6` names both passing configurations.
- Sections VI–X contain no typing/fading sequence. Many write labs reduce a domain concept to small list/dict/string formatting rather than exercising the project's real API, parser, test, or failure boundary.

**Impact**

Learners can exploit position and template regularities, while hints often turn retrieval into transcription. Later sections ask for sophisticated system judgment without a corresponding rise in authentic, independently produced code evidence.

**Remediation**

Redistribute correct choices, vary exercise order, and review distractors after every reorder. Rewrite hints as one-step prompts that preserve the solve. Add supported completion, bug diagnosis, schema/manifest validation, and small project-native tests before independent Working tasks. Interleave earlier budgeting, privacy, provenance, and failure-handling concepts instead of testing only the current lesson.

#### M2. The lesson bodies need more visual and cognitive variation

**Evidence**

- All 53 lessons include field notes and almost all include code, which is strong.
- No lesson body uses an HTML ordered or unordered list. Thirty-seven have no callout, while `sections/s02/lessons/l01.toml` has two callouts despite the one-callout maximum.
- Rough word counts including code place `s10-l02` and `s10-l03` just under the 300-word floor, while several lessons are above 600 and `s05-l04` reaches roughly 750 tokens/words under a simple text count. Stored `body_words` values should not be trusted as proof of current length.

**Impact**

Dense paragraph/code/field-note repetition makes long technical sections harder to scan, and the same visual rhythm reinforces the repetitive exercise rhythm. Overlong lessons also carry more unsupported claims and answer leakage.

**Remediation**

Bring each lesson to a genuinely useful 300–600-word body after stripping markup, not by padding. Use short lists, compact comparison tables, or a single purposeful callout where structure materially helps. Split overloaded conceptual work along functional seams, especially the rank/alpha/lr lesson, and remove the extra Section II callout.

#### M3. Evaluation thresholds and self-scoring overstate the available evidence

**Evidence**

- The course uses small 10-, 15-, and 20-case suites and a learner-scored 0–2 voice rubric to support claims about voice gain, regression, retrieval, and policy agreement.
- Blind comparison is taught, but there is no random-order protocol, scorer calibration, repeated scoring, second-reviewer option, or uncertainty treatment.
- Thresholds such as hit-rate@4 ≥ 0.80, policy agreement ≥ 0.85, and score tolerances are often presented as decision gates without showing how sample size, category risk, or baseline variance justified them.
- Critical-failure zero gates and one-variable comparisons are good design choices and should remain.

**Impact**

The reports can look quantitative while remaining sensitive to a single case, learner expectation, and rubric drift. They are useful smoke tests, but insufficient evidence for broad claims of generalization or safety.

**Remediation**

Describe these suites as predeclared starter acceptance tests, not comprehensive proof. Randomize blind presentation, preserve frozen case IDs, add repeat scoring or a second reviewer where practical, report category denominators and exact failures, and state uncertainty/limitations. Calibrate tolerances against repeated baseline runs and weight zero-tolerance categories by actual harm. Keep critical gates essential even when aggregate scores are high.

#### M4. The privacy curriculum does not cover the full personal-data lifecycle

**Evidence**

- `sections/s04/lessons/l05.toml` correctly says regex triage is not a privacy guarantee and requires manual review.
- The demonstrated patterns are narrow, and the course does not systematically address personally identifying information, confidential third-party content, attachments, ownership/licensing, source deletion, backups, version-control history, model extraction, or adapter disposal.
- “Own writing or personally rewrote” does not establish that all embedded names, business facts, client text, or quoted material may safely be used for training.

**Impact**

Learners may produce a technically clean dataset that still contains material they lack permission to train on or cannot later remove from derivatives.

**Remediation**

Add a data-governance checklist covering ownership/consent, PII and confidential entities, attachments, source provenance, retention, backups, deletion propagation, and derivative artifacts. Require a non-sensitive rejection ledger, a documented manual-review sample, and memorization/extraction probes. Connect this lesson explicitly to the project/snapshot boundary in C1 and make its critical conditions essential in Section IV.

#### M5. Several current-source and terminology details need correction

**Evidence**

- `sections/s04/lessons/l01.toml` and its hint direct the learner to Notion's old “Settings & Members” path. The current [Notion export instructions](https://www.notion.com/help/export-your-content) place whole-workspace export under `Settings` → workspace `General` → `Export all workspace content`.
- The lesson links a general workspace settings page rather than the direct export procedure.
- `sections/s02/lessons/l01.toml` uses plausible-looking `qwen3:8b-instruct-q4_K_M` and `qwen3:8b-base-q4_K_M` strings as toy ledger data without identifying them as synthetic examples.
- `sections/s05/lessons/l01.toml` says “The seventh rite begins” in Section V. Nearby “fifth rite” references are also difficult to map because “rite” alternates between lesson, section, and prior calculation.

**Impact**

Learners can search for nonexistent model tags, fail to locate a current UI control, or lose trust in cross-references. Mutable UI and tool documentation make these defects recur unless the course records what was verified.

**Remediation**

Update the Notion path and direct reading, label invented identifiers as synthetic, and replace ambiguous ordinal rite references with stable section/lesson names. During the technical correction pass, verify every operational link against a primary source and prefer versioned documentation for pinned software.
